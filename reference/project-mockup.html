Blazar variability with CHIME

Watching hundreds of jets pointed straight at us, every day, at radio frequencies nobody has monitored at this scale before.

01Why CHIME

A blazar is a galaxy with a jet pointed almost straight at us, so its brightness can swing a lot. Most monitoring has been done at higher radio frequencies. CHIME sees the whole northern sky every day, which makes it a good instrument for watching many blazars at once below 1 GHz. [Rewrite in your own words.]

02How variable are they?

To compare sources, I use the modulation index: the scatter in a source's flux density divided by its mean.

m= σS⟨S⟩

Here σS is the standard deviation of the flux density and ⟨S⟩ its mean. I compare the distribution of m between BL Lacs and FSRQs, and run a redshift-split null test. [Add the result in a sentence.]

03Hunting for periods

Some blazars may brighten on a regular cycle. I search for this with a weighted wavelet Z-transform (WWZ) and the Jurkevich method, and test significance against simulated red-noise light curves fit with PSRESP.
